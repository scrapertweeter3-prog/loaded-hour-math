The senior developer hour you are not counting costs $229

Salary math gets quoted as a base number, which is the smallest line on the invoice. Glassdoor puts the average US senior software engineer at about $18,290 a month fully loaded: base pay, then benefits at 35 percent, payroll taxes at 15, bonus at 15, and retirement at 8 layered on top. Recruiting and equipment add something like $13,500 before the first commit. Annualized, that is $219,480 in salary and $232,980 once onboarding costs land, and the invoice arrives whether the feature ships or not.

Utilization decides the real price of an hour. A salaried engineer is paid for roughly 173 hours a month, and meetings, context switching, and internal ceremony keep the hours that produce working code well below that. Assume an optimistic 80 delivered hours a month and the loaded hour is $229. A flat-fee subscription prices the same work differently: $5,995 a month buys unlimited requests, worked one active request at a time against a 72-hour turnaround target, so the same 80 delivered hours come out to about $75 apiece. The monthly spread is $12,295, or $147,540 a year. The Poket Dev unlimited development subscription prices that stack in the open, and its hire-versus-subscribe breakdown walks the full benefits-and-taxes comparison.

Onboarding shapes the difference too. A new hire spends the first weeks on access requests and standups before producing anything, while the subscription route takes about 24 hours to get requests onto a Trello board, runs Monday to Friday with no meetings, and a canceled plan keeps every line of code already delivered. For a team weighing a senior requisition against an unlimited-request subscription, the delivered hour is the number to price: $229 against $75 at the same utilization.
